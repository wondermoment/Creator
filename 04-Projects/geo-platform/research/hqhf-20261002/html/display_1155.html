Manifold 管路焊后表面处理技术及工艺

发布日期：2026-09-28 浏览次数：25

摘要

Manifold（液冷歧管）是数据中心冷板式与浸没式液冷系统的核心分流部件，通常由 316L/304L 不锈钢经氩弧焊或激光焊拼焊成型，内腔直接承载去离子水–乙二醇冷却液。焊接热循环会破坏焊区钝化膜、诱发晶间贫铬，并造成表面氧化与粗糙度恶化，直接威胁部件长期服役中的耐蚀性、洁净度与零泄漏可靠性。本文系统阐述 Manifold 焊后表面处理的核心目标与工艺难点，对比酸洗钝化与电解抛光两条技术路线的原理特点、适用场景及质量控制要求，为数据中心液冷部件的洁净制造提供工程参考。

关键词：Manifold；液冷歧管；焊后表面处理；酸洗钝化；电解抛光；洁净度控制

一、技术应用背景

随着 AI 算力密度持续攀升，数据中心冷却方式正从传统风冷向液冷加速迭代。作为液冷系统的"血管枢纽"，Manifold 负责将冷却液均匀分配至每个机柜与冷板，其内部流道的表面状态直接决定流阻特性、颗粒脱落水平、离子析出速率与长期运行可靠性。

典型 Manifold 由 316L 或 304L 不锈钢管材与板材经氩弧焊或激光焊拼焊成多流道一体结构，焊后不可避免地存在氧化色层、焊渣飞溅、内毛刺及热影响区贫铬带。焊后表面处理须实现以下四大目标：

1. 恢复焊区钝化膜：重建不锈钢表面富 Cr₂O₃ 防护层；

2. 抑制晶间腐蚀：消除敏化热影响区的贫铬晶界；

3. 降低内外表面粗糙度：减少颗粒挂壁与微生物附着；

4. 控制颗粒与离子析出：满足头部客户氦检漏率 <1×10⁻⁹ mbar·L/s、颗粒洁净度 <50 μm 的准入要求。

依据客户等级与应用场景，可分别选用酸洗钝化或电解抛光两条技术路线。

二、焊后表面处理的核心难点

不锈钢焊后表面处理并非简单的"酸洗即可"，而是面临多重耦合难点。

（一）焊区氧化皮与热变色去除

氩弧焊热输入较大，焊道及热影响区会形成蓝灰色至深褐色的氧化色层，主要由 Fe₃O₄、Cr₂O₃ 及尖晶石型复合氧化物构成。该氧化层结构致密、与基体结合牢固，常规酸洗难以彻底清除。

（二）晶间腐蚀敏感性

304L/316L 焊接热影响区在 450～850 ℃ 敏化区间停留时，Cr₂₃C₆ 沿晶界析出导致晶界贫铬，在含氯环境中极易诱发晶间腐蚀。焊后须按 ASTM A262 Practice A 进行草酸浸蚀检验，确认无晶界沟槽。

（三）游离铁污染控制

不锈钢加工与焊接过程中，工具铁屑及碳钢磨粒可能嵌入表面形成游离铁源，在冷却液环境中迅速引发点蚀与锈斑。因此，全过程禁止使用碳钢刷及普通钢丝工具，最终须以铁氰化钾蓝点试验验证无游离铁残留。

（四）内表面洁净度与颗粒控制

Manifold 内腔结构复杂、流道狭长，处理后残留的酸液、金属屑及磨料颗粒会在液冷系统中长期循环，堵塞冷板微通道或污染冷却液。终洗须采用去离子水（电导率 ≤1 μS/cm），并进行颗粒计数，满足 ISO 16232 / VDA 19 洁净度等级要求。

三、表面处理工艺方案

（一）前处理：焊后清洁与保护

前处理是保证钝化或电解抛光效果的前提，须按序完成四道工序：

1. 清渣去氧化皮：采用不锈钢丝刷或尼龙磨片清除焊渣、飞溅及厚重氧化皮，严禁使用碳钢工具；

2. 脱脂清洗：使用无硅、无氯清洗剂去除油污与机加工冷却液残留，禁用含氯溶剂以防点蚀；

3. 工装遮蔽：对密封面、螺纹及快插接口实施遮蔽，防止处理后尺寸超差；

4. 内腔检查：目视确认内腔无金属屑及可见异物。

（二）方案 A：酸洗钝化（常规项目）

酸洗钝化适用于一般工业级液冷 Manifold，核心作用是去除表面游离铁与贫铬氧化层，促使不锈钢表面重新生成富 Cr₂O₃ 钝化膜。工艺依据 ASTM A967 及 ASTM A380 标准执行，处理后以去离子水彻底冲洗至中性，再经洁净热风干燥。

酸洗钝化后外观应呈均匀银白色或哑光，无彩虹色热变色、无锈斑及酸液残留。该路线投资省、节拍快，是常规工况下的首选方案。

（三）方案 B：电解抛光（EP，高端升级）

电解抛光适用于头部客户、高洁净度要求或长寿命设计的 Manifold。EP 以专用酸系电解液为介质，对工件表面实施微观电化学整平：与机械抛光不同，EP 在溶解微观峰顶的同时去除表面畸变层，可获得半镜面至镜面光洁表面。

工艺控制要点如下：

l EP 单边去除量须严格受控，不得削弱管壁或密封面强度；

l 处理后内表面 Ra ≤0.8 μm、外表面 Ra ≤1.6 μm；

l 后处理流程：逐级去离子水冲洗（终水电导率 ≤1 μS/cm）→ 中和 → 超纯水终洗 → 洁净干燥；

l 处理后须复核密封面、螺纹及快插接口尺寸，确保落在图纸公差范围内。

Surface Treatment Technology and Process for Welded Manifold Piping

Abstract

The manifold is the core flow-distribution component of cold-plate and immersion liquid-cooling systems in data centers. It is fabricated from 316L/304L stainless steel by TIG or laser welding, and its internal channels directly carry deionized water–glycol coolant. The welding thermal cycle destroys the passivation film in the weld zone, induces intergranular chromium depletion, and degrades the surface finish—directly compromising long-term corrosion resistance, cleanliness, and zero-leakage reliability. This paper presents the core objectives and key challenges of post-weld surface treatment for manifolds, compares acid pickling passivation and electropolishing in terms of principles, application scenarios, and quality-control requirements, and provides an engineering reference for clean manufacturing of data-center liquid-cooling components.

Keywords: manifold; liquid cooling; post-weld surface treatment; pickling passivation; electropolishing; cleanliness control

1. Technical Application Background

Driven by the continuous surge in AI computing density, data-center cooling is shifting rapidly from traditional air cooling to liquid cooling. As the "vascular hub" of the liquid-cooling system, the manifold distributes coolant uniformly to every rack and cold plate; the surface condition of its internal channels directly determines flow resistance, particle shedding, ion leaching rate, and long-term operational reliability.

A typical manifold is fabricated from 316L or 304L stainless-steel tubing and plate by TIG or laser welding into an integrated multi-channel structure, which inevitably leaves heat tint, weld spatter, internal burrs, and chromium-depleted bands in the heat-affected zone. Post-weld surface treatment must achieve four objectives:

1. Restore the passivation film in the weld zone by rebuilding the Cr₂O₃-rich protective layer;

2. Suppress intergranular corrosion by eliminating chromium-depleted grain boundaries in the sensitized heat-affected zone;

3. Reduce internal and external surface roughness to minimize particle adhesion and microbial attachment;

4. Control long-term particle shedding and ion leaching to meet leading customers' requirements of helium leak rate <1×10⁻⁹ mbar·L/s and particle cleanliness <50 μm.

Depending on the customer grade and application scenario, two technical routes are available: acid pickling passivation and electropolishing.

2. Core Challenges of Post-Weld Surface Treatment

Post-weld surface treatment of stainless steel is by no means a trivial "pickling-and-done" operation; it involves several coupled challenges.

2.1 Removal of weld oxide scale and heat tint

TIG welding involves high heat input, forming blue-gray to dark-brown oxide layers on the weld bead and heat-affected zone, composed mainly of Fe₃O₄, Cr₂O₃, and spinel-type complex oxides. These layers are dense and tightly bonded to the substrate, making thorough removal by conventional pickling difficult.

2.2 Intergranular corrosion susceptibility

When the 304L/316L heat-affected zone dwells in the 450–850 °C sensitization range, Cr₂₃C₆ precipitates along grain boundaries and depletes chromium, rendering the material highly susceptible to intergranular corrosion in chloride-containing environments. Post-weld oxalic acid etching per ASTM A262 Practice A is mandatory to confirm the absence of grain-boundary grooves.

2.3 Free-iron contamination control

During machining and welding, tool iron particles and carbon-steel grit may embed in the surface, creating free-iron sites that rapidly initiate pitting and rust stains in the coolant environment. Carbon-steel brushes and ordinary steel-wire tools are strictly prohibited throughout the process, and final verification by the potassium ferricyanide blue-spot test must confirm no free-iron residue.

2.4 Internal cleanliness and particle control

Manifolds feature complex internal geometry and long, narrow flow channels; residual acid, metal chips, and abrasive particles after treatment would circulate indefinitely in the liquid-cooling loop, potentially blocking cold-plate microchannels or contaminating the coolant. Final rinsing must use deionized water (conductivity ≤1 μS/cm) with particle counting to meet ISO 16232 / VDA 19 cleanliness requirements.

3. Surface Treatment Process Schemes

3.1 Pretreatment: post-weld cleaning and protection

Pretreatment is the prerequisite for effective passivation or electropolishing and must be completed in the following sequence:

1. Slag and scale removal: remove weld spatter and heavy oxide scale with stainless-steel wire brushes or nylon abrasive pads; carbon-steel tools are strictly prohibited;

2. Degreasing: remove oil and machining-coolant residue with silicone-free, chloride-free detergents; chloride-containing solvents are banned to prevent pitting;

3. Masking: mask sealing surfaces, threads, and quick-connect fittings with dedicated tooling to prevent dimensional deviation after treatment;

4. Internal inspection: visually confirm the absence of metal chips and visible foreign matter in internal channels.

3.2 Scheme A: acid pickling passivation (standard)

Acid pickling passivation is suitable for general industrial-grade liquid-cooling manifolds. Its core function is to remove surface free iron and chromium-depleted oxide layers, regenerating a Cr₂O₃-rich passivation film on the stainless-steel surface. The process is performed in accordance with ASTM A967 and ASTM A380, followed by thorough deionized-water rinsing to neutrality and clean hot-air drying. After treatment, the appearance should be uniformly silver-white or matte, free of rainbow heat tint, rust spots, and acid residue. Low capital investment and fast cycle time make this route the preferred choice for standard service conditions.

3.3 Scheme B: electropolishing (EP, premium)

Electropolishing is recommended for manifolds serving leading customers, high-cleanliness applications, or long-life designs. EP employs a specialized acid-based electrolyte to perform microscopic electrochemical leveling of the workpiece surface. Unlike mechanical polishing, EP dissolves microscopic peaks while simultaneously removing the surface deformed layer, yielding a semi-mirror to mirror finish. Key process controls:

l The unilateral EP removal amount must be strictly controlled without compromising tube-wall or sealing-surface strength;

l After treatment, internal surface Ra ≤0.8 μm and external surface Ra ≤1.6 μm are required;

l Post-treatment sequence: cascaded deionized-water rinsing (final water conductivity ≤1 μS/cm) → neutralization → ultrapure-water final rinse → clean drying;

l After EP, the dimensions of sealing surfaces, threads, and quick-connect fittings must be re-verified to remain within drawing tolerances.

5. Typical Application Scenarios

1. Data-center liquid-cooling manifolds: cold-plate and immersion cooling distribution piping, supporting PUE <1.1 efficient heat dissipation and 7×24 h uninterrupted operation;

6. Conclusion

Post-weld surface treatment of manifolds is the critical process that elevates data-center liquid-cooling systems from merely "functional" to "reliable, long-life, and high-cleanliness". Pickling passivation economically restores the passivation film and removes free iron for standard industrial requirements, while electropolishing achieves low roughness, high cleanliness, and superior passivation simultaneously through microscopic electrochemical leveling—representing the upgrade path for premium liquid-cooling components. Hefei Huaqing Hi-Tech offers full-process capabilities spanning post-weld pretreatment, pickling passivation, and electropolishing, with flexible process selection tailored to customer grade, cleanliness requirements, and budget, delivering stable, compliant, and traceable surface-treatment solutions for stainless-steel welded piping in data-center liquid cooling, energy storage, hydrogen energy, and related fields.
